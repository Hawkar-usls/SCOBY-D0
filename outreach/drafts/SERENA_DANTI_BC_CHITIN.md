Your work on bacterial-cellulose meshes coated with chitin nanofibrils is particularly relevant to our effort to keep material identity and reinforcement claims experimentally separated. Our current framework treats chitin-containing reinforcement as a hypothesis until chitin identity, loading, spatial distribution, interface behavior and matched mechanical effects are measured.

The first controlled branch uses characterized exogenous chitin against an untreated BC baseline; biological or unconventional source materials are not allowed to bypass the same identity and composition gates.

If you are willing, I would greatly appreciate a brief critique of the proposed sequence: composition/identity → microstructure/distribution → interface → dry/wet mechanics → reproducibility. Are there specific characterization methods or controls you would consider indispensable before concluding that chitin truly reinforces a BC matrix rather than merely coating or co-depositing with it?

This is exploratory research, and no reinforcement or biomedical claim is currently made.
