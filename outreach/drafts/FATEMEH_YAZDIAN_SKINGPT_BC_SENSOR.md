I am developing an open bacterial-cellulose research platform called SCOBY-D0. One branch connects BC-derived materials to an experimental tactile/thermal sensor-skin program.

Sensor-skin track: https://github.com/Hawkar-usls/SCOBY-D0/blob/main/research_tracks/SKINGPT_SENSOR_SKIN/TRACK.json

I found your 2026 review on bacterial cellulose as a sustainable nanostructured polymer for biosensor development very useful for framing this branch. We are deliberately treating BC first as a material/substrate candidate rather than assuming that it is already a functional sensor.

Our promotion ladder requires material identity and process history, followed by mechanical/moisture characterization, then electrical/dielectric or transduction measurements, repeatability, drift/hysteresis and finally application-specific validation.

I would be grateful for your view on the minimum characterization set you would require before calling a BC-derived layer a credible tactile or thermal sensing substrate. In particular, I am interested in which controls best separate changes caused by humidity, deformation and temperature from the intended sensor response.

The branch is currently research-stage and no device-performance claim is made.
