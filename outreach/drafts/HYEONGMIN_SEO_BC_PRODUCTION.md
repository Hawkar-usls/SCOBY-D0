I was excited to find that your Engineered BioSynthesis Lab is explicitly working on improving bacterial fiber production through metabolic engineering of BC-producing bacteria. One of the central problems in our project is similar at a more exploratory level: separating reproducible BC production from assumptions about substrate identity, acetate/C2 metabolism, or downstream material performance.

Our framework requires process history, matched controls, production yield, structure and downstream properties to be recorded separately; simulations and chemical equivalence cannot be promoted to biological validation.

If you have time for a brief look, I would especially value your opinion on what minimum measurements would make an exploratory SCOBY-derived BC production screen biologically useful to a metabolic-engineering group. For example, which culture variables, yield metrics, strain/community measurements or carbon-flow measurements would you consider essential before interpreting a change in pellicle yield mechanistically?

The project is early R&D and negative results are explicitly preserved.
